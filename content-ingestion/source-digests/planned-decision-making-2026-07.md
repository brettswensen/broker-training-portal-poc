## Summary

Training on decision-making frameworks adapted from fighter pilot methodology. Introduces the OODA loop and FACADE model as tools for making better decisions under stress in real estate transactions. Based on Hazard Lee's book "The Art of Clear Thinking."

## Key Broker Guidance

### Core Principle
- "There is no situation so bad you can't make it worse"
- Rushed decisions often compound problems
- Goal: identify what makes it worse vs. better, choose wisely

### The OODA Loop
1. **Observe** - gather raw data and real-time information
2. **Orient** - build accurate mental picture of reality (NOT assumptions)
3. **Decide** - choose between good/better/best or bad/worse/awful
4. **Act** - execute, then observe results and repeat

### Gathering Information
- Phone calls: immediate feedback but easily misremembered
- Email/text: written record but time lag
- Use open-ended questions, listen more than talk
- Ask: What do we know for sure? What are we assuming?

### Danger of Assumptions
- 95% of reality is not enough in critical situations
- F-35 stall example: pilot assumed "close enough" parameters, stalled at 5 miles up
- Verify facts before acting

### Managing Stress
- Clients often in "autopilot" until crisis, then panic
- Help them move to alert readiness without panic
- Acknowledge stress, then systematically work through OODA

### FACADE Model (for complex decisions)
- **F**ocus - identify specific problem
- **A**ssess - gather relevant information
- **C**onsider - evaluate options and consequences
- **A**ct - choose and execute
- **D**ecide - commit to the path
- **E**valuate - review the outcome

## Objections and Responses

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| We don't have time to think | Even 5 minutes of observation prevents costly mistakes |
| I already know what to do | Every situation is unique; quick orientation check confirms path |
| Other party is being unreasonable | Separate facts from assumptions about motives; gather more data |

## Summary

Craig leads a training session on repair negotiations, covering strategies from both the buyer and listing agent perspectives. The core framework is the "Three Buckets" approach: Safety/Health items, Big Ticket items, and Cosmetic items. The training emphasizes aggressive but ethical negotiation, upfront disclosure strategies for listings, and specific scripting that gets results. ## Key Broker Guidance

### The Three Buckets Framework

**1. Safety/Health (Highest Priority)**
- Electrical issues (double-tapped wires, ungrounded outlets, old panels with known failure rates, aluminum wiring)
- Actively leaking plumbing (mold/structural risk)
- Galvanized water lines (even if working - "you'll need to disclose to next buyer")
- Sewer mainline issues (when scoped)
- Structural/safety issues (wobbly deck = $12K negotiation)

**2. Big Ticket Items**
- Old roof, HVAC, furnace, water heater
- Best negotiated UPFRONT at offer stage by asking ages during showing
- If seller misrepresents age, use as post-inspection leverage
- Offer 2-year home warranty as compromise

**3. Cosmetic (Don't Bother)**
- Cracks in concrete (100% visible at walkthrough)
- Carpet condition (visible before offer)
- Negative slope (common on older homes)
- Trees (unless power line safety issue)

### Buyer Agent Scripting

**Strong Opening Language:**
> "These are significant safety concerns that could cause the buyer to withdraw."

**Permission to Be Aggressive:**
> "Can I be aggressive and give the indication to the seller that these are concerning enough to you that you may back out?"

**When Seller Misrepresented Ages:**
> "We offered under the assumption the furnace was 5 years old. Turns out it's 15. We did not anticipate that."

### Listing Agent Defense

**Upfront Disclosure Strategy:**
- Put system ages (roof, HVAC, water heater) in AGENT REMARKS
- When buyer requests repairs later: "That was known upfront - right there in the listing details"

**Age-Appropriate Expectations:**
> "You're buying a 1950s home. Some stuff will be new, some won't. We didn't advertise this as brand new."

**Compromise Offer:**
> "We'll buy you a 2-year home warranty."

### Contractor Credits (Critical)

Credits going directly to contractors do NOT count as seller contribution limits:
- Can have 20% of home value go to contractors
- Name contractor on settlement statement
- Doesn't show on buyer's settlement statement
- Doesn't influence underwriting
- Example: "$18,000 credit to Malachi Roofing" on seller side only

## Client-Facing Language

**For Safety Items:**
> "These are significant safety and health concerns to the degree that the buyer would back out if not addressed."

**For Old Systems:**
> "These are all old. They're all working according to the seller, but it's not likely for us to get brand new everything. What is likely is a warranty."

**For Listing Defense:**
> "We put the specific year in the agent remarks so this would be avoided if you were to come back at this moment."

## Search Chunks

### chunk-001
**Timestamp:** 00:00:59-00:01:57
**Topic tags:** repair negotiations, safety items, buyer representation
**Searchable excerpt:** From the buyer's side, the things that are most successful to negotiate fall into buckets. Bucket number one is safety. When you focus negotiations on safety items, it becomes easier. If you phrase it like 'the buyer would really like this,' sellers will beat you up. Instead say: 'These are significant safety concerns that could cause the buyer to withdraw.'

### chunk-002
**Timestamp:** 00:01:57-00:02:49
**Topic tags:** negotiation language, seller's market, buyer's market
**Searchable excerpt:** Use strong verbiage: 'Will cause the buyer to withdraw if not satisfied.' When you say something along those lines, it makes it come across stronger in the negotiation. We're in a buyer's market so it's easier, but if these principles work in a seller's market they'll work in any market.

### chunk-003
**Timestamp:** 00:02:49-00:04:43
**Topic tags:** electrical safety, plumbing, old panels, aluminum wiring
**Searchable excerpt:** Electrical items are no brainers in the safety bucket. Plumbing leaks and electrical issues. On older homes you might have electrical that's completely working but it's an older panel with known failure rates. Look up failure rates - some panels are known to fail. Good enough to say 'Here is a safety issue. We want this panel replaced.'

### chunk-004
**Timestamp:** 00:04:43-00:06:37
**Topic tags:** galvanized plumbing, sewer scope, disclosure
**Searchable excerpt:** Galvanized water lines - even if working, you can negotiate. Argument: 'You will need to disclose these items to the next buyer.' Sewer mainline - pretty easy negotiation if scoped. Always recommend getting sewer scopes.

### chunk-005
**Timestamp:** 00:06:37-00:15:55

**Searchable excerpt:** Old HVAC, furnace, water heater - best negotiated UPFRONT before inspection. Ask ages during showing. If old, go back to client: 'These are all old. It's not likely to get brand new stuff. What is likely is a warranty.'

### chunk-006
**Timestamp:** 00:15:55-00:21:27
**Topic tags:** misrepresentation, multiple offers, price reduction
**Searchable excerpt:** If seller says furnace is 5 years old and inspection shows it's 15, you have clear runway: 'We offered under the assumption it was 5. Turns out it's 15.' In multiple offer situation, don't ask for ages upfront - get under contract first.

### chunk-007
**Timestamp:** 00:07:36-00:09:26
**Topic tags:** meth testing, health hazards, state limits
**Searchable excerpt:** Meth testing: State number is 1.0 on the divided-out number. Aggregate number on left, divided number in middle. Middle number is what state cares about. Below 1.0 = trace amount. Can sometimes get cleanses done on trace amounts.

### chunk-008
**Timestamp:** 00:09:26-00:13:03
**Topic tags:** meth negotiation, HVAC, trace amounts
**Searchable excerpt:** Focus meth negotiation on HVAC/furnace system - that's where traces congregate. If aggregate exceeds 1.0 but less than state limit, push for cleanse. If near 1.0, still try. Coming in at 0.4 - trace so low buyers aren't scared.

### chunk-014
**Timestamp:** 00:40:43-00:42:29
**Topic tags:** fiduciary duty, inspection reports, agency law
**Searchable excerpt:** Listing agent is under fiduciary responsibility to provide inspection report to seller. What agent knows, principal knows. Not forwarding opens them to liability. Next buyer must be given that report.

### chunk-015
**Timestamp:** 00:42:29-00:45:12
**Topic tags:** written negotiations, lender requirements, verbal agreements
**Searchable excerpt:** If buyer submits 5 repairs and seller will only do 3, don't counter in writing. Lender will see unfixed items. Let addendum lapse, agree verbally on 3, then resubmit ONLY those 3.

## Summary

Training on practical contract administration errors that agents frequently make with the Utah REPC. Covers earnest money handling, included items disputes, math errors, settlement vs closing confusion, HOA fees, compensation structure pitfalls, PID disclosures, and early possession disasters.

## Key Broker Guidance

### Earnest Money
- Wire transfers: $15-20 fee, not received until receipt confirmed
- Plan ahead for weekends/holidays
- One deposit cannot cover multiple contracts without written addendum
- Cashier's checks: acceptable but lenders may frown on seasoning verification

### Included Items
- REPC already covers: window coverings, curtains, rods, blinds, shutters, screen/storm doors, awnings
- Do NOT redundantly list in "Other Included Items"
- Mounted = included unless specifically excluded
- House must be delivered in condition it was in when under contract

### Math and Blanks
- Double-check all math, especially seller financing
- Put zeros in unused fields for visual clarity
- Settlement vs closing: up to 4-day gap possible
- Avoid Friday signings when possible

### HOA Fees
- Two fees: reinvestment fee (operating account) + transfer fee (paperwork)
- Utah limits reinvestment fees for HOAs established in last 3 years
- Older HOAs may charge up to 1.5% of purchase price
- State now has HOA Ombudsman for disputes

### Compensation Structure
- NEVER leave zero in unused field
- Use "does not apply" in the unused option
- Litigation risk: attorney successfully argued "percentage OR zero dollars" meant buyer could opt for zero
- Applies to REPC, Buyer Broker Agreement, and ERS

### PID Disclosure (Critical)
- Public Improvement District = separate taxing district
- Can add $45-50K over 20 years
- MUST verify and disclose for new construction
- All subsequent owners must also disclose

### Appraisal Issues
- Can renegotiate, request reappraisal, or buyer makes up difference
- Document errors carefully (solar panels, square footage, etc.)
- Lender supervisors can approve new appraisals on conventional loans

### Early Possession
- NEVER allow without indemnity agreement
- Horror story: buyers remodeled before closing, racked up credit card debt, lost loan qualification
- If absolutely necessary: indemnity agreement + supervise carefully

## Objections and Responses

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| Title company said one deposit covers multiple contracts | Each contract is separate; need written addendum or sellers could sue |
| Seller took curtains that "weren't included" | Mounted items are included by default; seller must replace or compensate |
| Why can't I move in early? | Huge liability; seen buyers lose qualification and damage property |

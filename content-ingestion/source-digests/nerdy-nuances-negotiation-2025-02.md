Topics include earnest money leverage, delayed possession vs leaseback, assessment sections, hidden second due diligence deadline, new construction completion notices, deadline reminder strategy, response deadline clarification, contingent cancellation addendum, escalation clauses, and the power of goodwill.

## Key Broker Guidance

### Earnest Money Leverage (Buyer Side)
- Increase earnest money to 2-3% in competitive situations
- On $500K FHA purchase: suggest $10-15K instead of $5K
- Higher earnest money = more attractive offer + insurance if seller breaches for higher offer

### Earnest Money Due Date Leverage (Seller Side)
- If buyer misses earnest money deadline = breach of contract
- Cancel FIRST (before buyer cancels) to claim the deposit
- Case study: Heather's investors planned inspection renegotiation ($350K to $280K); missed earnest money deadline; seller canceled first and kept $5K

### Delayed Possession vs Leaseback
- **Buyers:** Use delayed possession — seller stays responsible for property condition
- **Sellers:** Use leaseback — transfers responsibility to buyer/landlord
- Case study: Carter's seller had $1,500 water damage after closing; delayed possession made seller pay

### Section 4.2 and 4.3C — Assessments
- Always mark "seller" — never "NA" (not defined in contract)
- No seller in current market will reject unless hiding an assessment

### Hidden Second Due Diligence Deadline (Section 8.3B(I))
- "Buyer's sole discretion" to cancel after DD deadline with $0 earnest money forfeited
- No documentation required
- Buyer agents: don't teach buyers about this; use as safety net only
- Seller agents: warn seller and consider countering for half earnest money

### New Construction — Substantial Completion Notice
- Builder must provide signed written notice per Sections 12.2 + 19
- Email C of O alone is NOT sufficient
- Don't proactively point this out; wait until builder tries to charge late fees

### Stop Reminding Other Side of Deadlines
- Your duty of loyalty is to YOUR client
- Heather's case: not reminding = $5K advantage when buyer missed deadline

### Section 25 — Acceptance Deadline (Not Response Deadline)
- There is NO response deadline in REPC
- Counteroffers don't need to meet acceptance deadline
- Changes must be accepted by both in writing

### Contingent Cancellation Addendum
- "Ultimatum addendum" — cancel unless terms met by deadline
- Protects earnest money
- Language is cold; always accompany with phone call/email explaining intent to keep deal alive

### Escalation Clauses
- No UAR-approved form; use attorney-drafted template
- Beat competing offers by $2,500 (not $1,000) with a cap
- Risk: Seller can counter straight to cap
- As listing agent: counter straight to cap

### Power of Goodwill
- Most effective negotiation tactic
- Assume other side wants to win; they're your counterpart, not adversary
- Exercise goodwill from start through closing
- When hiccups arise, goodwill gives your client advantage
